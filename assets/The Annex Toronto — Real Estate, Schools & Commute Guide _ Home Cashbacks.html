The Annex sits between Bathurst and Avenue Road, Bloor to Dupont. Victorian and Edwardian homes on tree-lined streets, two subway lines, and the University of Toronto on the eastern edge. It has the density of downtown with the feel of a real neighbourhood. That combination keeps demand steady regardless of what the broader market does.

Who buys here

Families
Strong public schools, walkable streets, parks nearby. Huron St PS is one of the most sought-after TDSB catchments in Toronto. Expect competition for the right homes.
Professionals
U of T faculty, Bay St commuters, and downtown workers who want character over glass. The subway commute is genuinely fast and the neighbourhood has everything walkable.
Move-up buyers
Buyers coming from condos elsewhere in Toronto who want a house with outdoor space and neighbourhood permanence. Often patient — they wait for the right property.
Not ideal for
First-time buyers on a tight budget. Entry-level detached is rare under $2M. Condos are more accessible but competition is real and the neighbourhood commands a premium.

Common questions

Is The Annex good for families? +
Yes. Strong TDSB public schools, walkable streets, parks, and community infrastructure. Huron St Junior PS is one of the most in-demand catchments in Toronto. Families who buy here tend to stay.
The Annex vs Roncesvalles — which is better value? +
Roncesvalles tends to offer slightly more space per dollar on freehold homes, with a similar walkable village feel. The Annex has better subway access and stronger school reputation. If transit is your priority, The Annex wins. If value per square foot matters more, look at Roncesvalles.
Is parking an issue in The Annex? +
Most detached and semi-detached homes have one parking space via a rear laneway. Condos vary — many do not include parking or charge extra for it. Street parking is permit-based. Most residents rely on transit and do not need a car day-to-day.
