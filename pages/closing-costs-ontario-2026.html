How much to budget for closing costs

A safe rule of thumb for Ontario buyers is 1.5% to 4% of the purchase price. Toronto buyers should use the higher end because they pay both provincial and municipal land transfer tax — which doubles the largest closing cost.

Land transfer tax — Ontario and Toronto

Land transfer tax (LTT) is the largest closing cost for most buyers. Ontario charges it on every property purchase. The City of Toronto charges an additional municipal land transfer tax on top — meaning Toronto buyers pay twice.

Toronto municipal land transfer tax

Toronto buyers pay the same rates again on top of provincial LTT. On a $1M home in Toronto, the combined land transfer tax is approximately $32,200 — before any rebates.

First-time buyer land transfer tax rebate

If you have never owned a home anywhere in the world, you qualify as a first-time buyer in Ontario and can claim rebates on both provincial and municipal LTT.

RebateMaximum amountWho qualifies
Ontario provincial LTT rebateUp to $4,000First-time buyers across Ontario
Toronto municipal LTT rebateUp to $4,475First-time buyers purchasing in Toronto
Combined Toronto first-time rebateUp to $8,475Toronto first-time buyers

The rebate is applied automatically at closing by your lawyer. You do not need to apply separately — but your lawyer must know you are a first-time buyer.

How to reduce closing costs

  • Claim all first-time buyer rebates — Make sure your lawyer knows you are a first-time buyer. The $8,475 combined rebate in Toronto is significant.
  • Use a cashback realtor — Getting $15,000 to $20,000 back at closing directly offsets your closing cost bill.
  • Shop legal fees — Real estate lawyers charge differently. Get two or three quotes. The work is largely standardized.
  • Review the property tax adjustment — Ensure you are not overpaying. Your lawyer handles this but it is worth understanding the calculation.
  • Buy outside Toronto if possible — Avoiding the municipal LTT alone saves $10,000 to $16,000 depending on price.

Common questions

Are closing costs included in the mortgage in Ontario? +
Generally no. Closing costs must be paid in cash on closing day. CMHC mortgage insurance premium is the exception — it can be added to the mortgage balance if you have less than 20% down. Everything else — land transfer tax, legal fees, insurance — must be liquid funds.
When are closing costs paid? +
All closing costs are due on the closing date — the day ownership transfers. Your lawyer will provide a closing statement a day or two before, showing the exact amounts. You transfer the funds to your lawyer in trust, and they distribute everything at closing.
Do condos have different closing costs? +
Condos have one additional cost: the status certificate review. This is a document from the condo corporation that discloses the building's financial health, any ongoing lawsuits, and the rules. Your lawyer should review it before you close. Budget $100 to $200 for the certificate itself, plus lawyer time to review it.
Are closing costs tax deductible in Canada? +
For a principal residence, no — closing costs are not tax deductible. If you are purchasing a rental property, some costs may be deductible or added to the adjusted cost base of the property. Consult a tax advisor for investment property purchases.
