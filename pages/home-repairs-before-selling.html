Home Repairs Before Selling

Not every renovation pays you back. Here's what actually moves the number in Toronto, and what to skip entirely.

The short answer

Small updates beat big renovations, almost every time

The instinct to fully renovate before selling is common, and usually wrong. Across Toronto and national data alike, small cosmetic updates consistently outperform major renovations on a dollar-for-dollar return basis. Your goal isn't a showpiece home, it's a market-ready one.

What to skip

Renovations that rarely pay you back

!
Full kitchen remodels Rarely returns its full cost immediately before selling. A refresh does the job at a fraction of the price.
!
Luxury bathroom overhauls Spa-like features feel personal to you, but buyers rarely pay full price for finishes they wouldn't have chosen.
!
Primary suite additions and major structural work Major, high-end additions often return only 24 to 36% of cost. These are lifestyle investments, not resale investments.
!
Backyard resort features, garage conversions, sunrooms Highly personal, expensive, and rarely valued by buyers the same way you value them.
Legal obligation

Disclosure is not optional in Ontario

Known latent defects, issues affecting a home's safety or habitability, must be disclosed to buyers under Ontario law, even when a home is sold as-is. A pre-listing home inspection is one of the most useful things a seller can do: it surfaces issues before a buyer's inspector finds them, giving you the choice to fix, disclose, or price around them on your own terms, instead of being surprised mid-negotiation.
