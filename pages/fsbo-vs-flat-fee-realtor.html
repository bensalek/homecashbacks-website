FSBO vs Flat Fee Realtor

Selling privately looks like it saves the full commission. Once you factor in what FSBO sellers still have to pay, the real gap is a lot smaller than it looks on paper.

The short answer

FSBO isn't free, it's just a different set of costs

Selling For Sale By Owner (FSBO) means listing and selling your home without a licensed agent representing you. It's legal in Ontario, no license is required to sell your own property. What most sellers don't realize going in is that FSBO doesn't actually eliminate the commission, it eliminates roughly half of it, while adding real time, risk, and out-of-pocket costs that a flat fee listing already includes.

What FSBO does and doesn't save
Saves: the listing agent's commission, typically 2.5%
Does not save: the buyer agent commission, still typically 2% to 2.5%
Does not save: a real estate lawyer, legally required either way
Does not save: photography, MLS access, staging, if you want real exposure

Why buyers pay less for FSBO homes

The bigger risk isn't the fee, it's the sale price

Multiple studies suggest FSBO homes sell for 15% to 30% less than comparable agent-assisted sales. On a $1,000,000 home, that is a $150,000 to $300,000 gap, far more than the commission a seller set out to save. A few reasons this happens consistently:

Buyers expect a discount from a private seller Buyers know you saved on commission and often negotiate as though they should share in it.
Pricing without current comparable sales data Agents have direct access to recent sold prices. Without it, FSBO sellers commonly misprice from the start.
A smaller pool of buyers Some buyer agents are hesitant to show FSBO listings, assuming more friction in the transaction.
Negotiation asymmetry Buyer agents negotiate for a living. Most FSBO sellers negotiate a home sale once a decade, if that.

FSBO still makes sense in a narrow set of cases, most often when the seller already has a buyer lined up, such as a sale to family or a known buyer, where the value an agent adds through pricing, marketing, and negotiation matters less.
