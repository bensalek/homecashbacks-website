Offer Conditions Explained

Every standard condition you can put on an offer, what each one actually protects you against, and real current timeframes, not vague estimates.

The short answer

A condition buys you time before you're locked in

A condition gives you a set number of business days to complete due diligence, securing financing, getting an inspection, reviewing documents, before the offer becomes a legally binding agreement. If a condition isn't satisfied or waived within that window, the offer becomes null and void and your deposit is returned. An offer with no conditions is called a firm offer, binding the moment it's accepted.

This has to be in writing, not a phone call
Waiving or satisfying a condition must be done in writing within the time period specified. A verbal agreement to drop a condition doesn't count. Without written notice, the offer can become null and void even if everyone assumed it was moving forward.
The standard set

Common conditions and real timeframes

ConditionTypical timeframeWhat it protects against
Financing3 to 7 business daysYour lender declining final approval on this specific property
Home inspection5 to 7 business daysStructural, mechanical, or safety issues you can't see
Status certificate (condo)3 to 5 business daysPoor building finances, litigation, upcoming special assessments
Sale of your current propertyVaries, often 30-60+ daysBeing unable to close because your own sale fell through
AppraisalOften bundled with financingThe lender's valuation coming in below your purchase price
Well/septic (rural)5 to 7 business daysA failing or non-compliant private water or septic system

Financing and inspection are by far the most common conditions in a typical Toronto offer. Status certificate review used to be the default for condos, but many buyers now review it before making an offer at all, since certificates are often available quickly online, which lets them skip including it as a condition entirely.

Worth knowing

The condo corporation has a legal deadline too

Once requested, a condo corporation has 10 days under Ontario law to provide the status certificate. This is part of why the timeline for this specific condition can feel tighter than others, your lawyer's actual review window depends on how quickly the corporation responds within that legal window.

The real trade-off

Every condition you include has a cost

Conditions protect you, but they also make your offer less attractive to a seller, particularly in a multiple-offer situation, since a conditional offer carries more risk of falling through than a firm one. This isn't a reason to drop every condition, it's a reason to be deliberate about which ones you actually need for a specific property versus which ones are just habit.
