What happens to the commission

When a seller lists on MLS, they specify a buyer agent commission — typically 2 to 2.5% of the sale price. This money goes to whoever represents the buyer at closing. It is set by the seller and exists in every deal regardless of which agent you choose.

The only question is who keeps it.

💡
Traditional agentKeeps 100% of the commission. On a $1M home at 2.5%, that is $25,000 the buyer never sees.
💰
Home CashbacksTakes $4,999 flat. Returns $20,000 to you after closing on that same $1M home. The seller pays the same either way.

Is the service different?

In terms of legal representation and scope — no. Both types of agent are licensed under RECO, bound by the same code of ethics, and provide the same core services: MLS access, showings, offer writing, negotiation, and closing coordination.

The one practical difference with Home Cashbacks: the first showing is free with no agreement required, and additional showings are $30 each. This keeps the model honest — buyers who are serious pay a small amount per showing, which funds genuinely useful visits rather than casual browsing at an agent's expense.

Everything else — offers, negotiation, market analysis, closing — is included at no extra cost.

Common questions

Why would anyone use a traditional realtor over a cashback realtor? +
Familiarity and trust. Many buyers work with agents referred by friends or family without questioning the commission structure. Others prefer an agent who has sold in their specific neighbourhood for 20 years. Both are valid reasons — but it is worth knowing what the alternative looks like before deciding.
Do traditional realtors negotiate better than cashback realtors? +
There is no evidence that commission size improves negotiation outcomes. A buyer's agent earns nearly the same whether your offer is accepted at $950K or $1.05M — the difference in their commission is a few hundred dollars, not a meaningful incentive. Good negotiation comes from market knowledge and strategy, not fee structure.
Can I switch from a traditional realtor to a cashback realtor? +
Yes, if your buyer representation agreement has expired or been cancelled by mutual consent. Check your agreement for the expiry date and holdover clause. If you are not yet under agreement, you can work with a cashback realtor immediately — start with a free showing, no commitment required.
