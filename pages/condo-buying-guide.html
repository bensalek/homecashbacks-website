Understand the structure

How a Condo Is Different

With a freehold home, you own the building and the land. With a condo, you own your unit and a share of everything else. That everything else is managed by a condo corporation to run by a board elected by unit owners.

The corporation sets your monthly fee, manages the reserve fund, and can charge you for unexpected costs. Its financial health directly affects yours.

You own
Your unit interior
Your appliances and finishes
Your parking spot and locker
Corporation manages
Lobby, hallways, amenities
Roof, elevators, mechanical systems
Reserve fund for major repairs
The key risk: If the corporation is poorly managed or underfunded, you can be hit with a special assessment to a one-time charge that can run tens of thousands of dollars. This is why you don't just buy a unit. You buy into a building.

Before you firm up

The Status Certificate

This document reveals the building's financial and legal health. Your lawyer reviews it before you firm up.

Three questions your lawyer must answer:

01
Is the reserve fund at least 70% funded?
Below that, a special assessment is likely. Factor it into your offer price or walk away.
02
Is there active litigation against the corporation?
Can block your mortgage approval with some lenders. Talk to your lawyer before proceeding.
03
Is there a pending special assessment?
Already approved but not yet collected means you pay it after closing. Quantify it first.
