Stigmatized Property Disclosure

Did something happen in this home? Here's the real legal answer in Ontario, and a genuine way to protect yourself if it matters to you.

The short answer

No, there's no legal requirement to disclose

Under Ontario common law and the principle of caveat emptor, buyer beware, a seller is generally not required to volunteer that a death occurred in a home, whether natural, accidental, or violent, and this applies whether or not the seller actually knows about it. Ontario is not alone here, every Canadian province except Quebec takes the same position.

What counts as a stigma
A death, natural or otherwise
A violent crime or murder
A former cannabis grow operation
A reputation for being haunted

A stigma is different from a physical defect. It doesn't affect how the home functions or whether it's safe, it's purely about how a buyer feels, which is exactly why the law treats it differently from something like knob and tube wiring or a leaking roof.

The nuance worth knowing

Agents aren't quite in the same position as sellers

The legal position for sellers is clear, but real estate professionals operate under an additional layer of ethical expectation. If your own agent knows about a stigma and knows it would matter to you, their duty to protect your interests generally means they should raise it. A seller's agent may be instructed by their client not to volunteer it, but if asked directly, they're required to answer truthfully rather than mislead you, since lying would cross into misrepresentation, a separate legal problem entirely.

How to actually protect yourself

Ask, and consider a clause in your offer

Ask directly if it matters to youA direct question must be answered truthfully, even though nobody is required to volunteer the information first.
Tell your agent it matters to youSay so early, so they know to raise it if they become aware of anything.
Ask about including a specific clause in your offerSome buyers include a warranty or condition addressing this directly, giving added protection if it turns out to matter later.
A real example of clause wording used in practice
"The Seller warrants and represents to the best of their knowledge and belief that there have been no deaths, murders, suicides, or unnatural violent injuries to persons in and about the property since the Seller acquired ownership."

This is genuinely worth discussing with your lawyer if a stigma is a real concern for you, since exact wording and its legal effect can vary. It's not a standard clause in every offer, but it exists, and it's a reasonable thing to ask for if this matters to you.
