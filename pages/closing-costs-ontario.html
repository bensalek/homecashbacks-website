How much to budget

A safe rule: 1.5% to 4% of the purchase price. Toronto buyers use the higher end — they pay both provincial and municipal land transfer tax, which roughly doubles the largest closing cost.

Land transfer tax — Ontario and Toronto

LTT is the largest closing cost for most buyers. Ontario charges it on every purchase. Toronto adds a second municipal tax on top — meaning Toronto buyers pay it twice.

Toronto buyers pay the same rates again as municipal LTT. On a $1M Toronto home the combined total is approximately $32,200 before rebates.

First-time buyer rebatesNever owned a home anywhere in the world? You get up to $4,000 off provincial LTT. Toronto first-time buyers get an additional $4,475 off municipal LTT. Combined: up to $8,475 back. Your lawyer applies this automatically at closing.

How to reduce closing costs

  • Claim your first-time buyer rebates — Tell your lawyer upfront. The $8,475 combined rebate in Toronto is not automatic without that conversation.
  • Use a cashback realtor — $15,000 to $20,000 back at closing directly offsets your bill.
  • Shop legal fees — The work is largely standardized. Get two or three quotes.
  • Buy outside Toronto — Avoiding the municipal LTT saves $10,000 to $16,000 depending on price.

Common questions

Are closing costs included in the mortgage? +
No. Closing costs must be paid in cash on closing day. CMHC mortgage insurance is the only exception — it can be added to your mortgage balance if you have less than 20% down.
When are closing costs paid? +
All closing costs are due on the closing date. Your lawyer provides a statement a day or two before showing exact amounts. You transfer funds to your lawyer in trust, and they distribute everything at closing.
Do condos have different closing costs? +
One addition: a status certificate review. This document from the condo corporation discloses the building's financial health, lawsuits, and rules. Budget $100 to $200 for the certificate plus lawyer time to review it.
Are closing costs tax deductible? +
For a principal residence, no. For a rental property, some costs may be deductible or added to the adjusted cost base. Consult a tax advisor for investment purchases.
