Flat Fee vs Percentage Cashback Realtor

A 50% cashback offer sounds generous — until you see what it actually returns on a Toronto-priced home.

The two cashback models

Cashback realtors in Ontario generally use one of two fee structures. Both are legal, both must be disclosed in writing, but they behave very differently as home prices change.

Percentage modelFlat fee model
How it works Realtor keeps a fixed % of commission (e.g. 50%), returns the rest Realtor charges a fixed dollar amount, returns everything above it
Realtor's earnings Grows as home price grows Stays the same regardless of price
Your cashback Grows slower than commission as price rises Grows at the same rate as the commission
Best for buyer when Home price is low and commission is small Home price is mid-to-high — most of the GTA

Where the flat fee starts winning

Using a common 50% cashback model as the comparison and a flat $4,999 fee, here is the price point where flat fee overtakes percentage:

Crossover point
~$450,000
Above this price, a flat $4,999 fee returns more cashback than a 50% split. Since the average GTA home price is over $1,000,000, the flat fee model wins on the vast majority of Toronto and GTA purchases.

The gap compounds with priceAt $1M the flat fee returns $7,501 more than the 50% model. At $2M that gap grows to $20,000. The higher the home price, the more a percentage model costs you.

Why the gap exists

A percentage model is designed so the realtor's earnings scale with the sale price — the same incentive structure traditional agents use, just with a smaller cut. On a $2M home, a realtor keeping 50% still earns $25,000. That is more than most flat-fee realtors charge for the entire transaction.

A flat fee model caps the realtor's earnings on purpose. The trade-off is that it only works at scale — a brokerage doing enough transactions can sustain a fixed low fee per file. This is why flat-fee models tend to come from full-time, higher-volume realtors rather than part-time agents.

💰
Neither model is inherently dishonestThe difference is simply math. A percentage model can make sense on lower-priced homes outside the GTA. In Toronto, where the average home price is over $1M, a flat fee almost always returns more.

Common questions

Is a percentage model ever better for the buyer? +
On homes below roughly $450,000, a 50% percentage split can return slightly more than a flat $4,999 fee, since the commission itself is small. Below the GTA's typical price range, it is worth comparing both models directly using the actual commission offered.
Do percentage models have other costs to watch for? +
Some percentage models add minimum fee clauses or per-showing charges on top of the percentage split, which can reduce the effective cashback further. Always ask for the complete fee structure in writing, regardless of which model a realtor uses.
Can a realtor change the model depending on the home price? +
Some brokerages do offer hybrid structures. What matters is that the exact fee or percentage is disclosed and written into your buyer representation agreement before any offer is made, regardless of which structure is used.
