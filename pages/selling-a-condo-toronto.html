Selling a Condo in Toronto

Different rules from selling a house. Here's what actually matters: pricing, staging a smaller space, and being ready with the one document buyers now expect upfront.

The short answer

Three things genuinely differ from selling a house

The core selling process is the same, but three things work differently for a condo: how it's priced, how it's staged, and one document buyers increasingly expect before they even offer, not after.

Pricing

Priced per square foot, not by lot

A condo is typically priced and compared against recently sold, similar units in the same or comparable buildings, on a price-per-square-foot basis. Land value and lot size, the usual drivers for a freehold price, don't factor in the same way. Getting an accurate comparable set from your building and nearby comparable buildings matters more here than general neighbourhood trends.

Staging

Smaller spaces need a different approach

Declutter aggressivelyEvery extra item reads as lost space in a smaller unit far more than it would in a house.
Maximize natural light and any viewViews and light are genuine selling points in a condo, make sure blinds and staging don't block them during showings.
Highlight the building's amenities, not just the unitBuyers are also evaluating the gym, concierge, and shared spaces as part of what they're paying for.
The document that increasingly matters upfront

Get your status certificate ready before you list

Buyers increasingly review a condo's status certificate before making an offer at all, rather than including it as a condition afterward. Requesting it early and having it ready to share can help your listing move faster and signals a well-prepared seller, rather than a delay once you're already in negotiations.

If your building has had a special assessment
Be upfront about what it addressed
Confirm the building's current financial health
Frame it as resolved, not ongoing, if it's paid off

A well-informed buyer researching the building will find this information anyway. Being upfront about it tends to land better than having it discovered.
