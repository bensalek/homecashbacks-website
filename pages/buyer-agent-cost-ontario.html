How buyer agent commission works in Ontario

When a seller lists a home on the MLS, they specify how much they are offering to pay a buyer's agent. This amount — called the buyer agent commission — is set before any offers come in and applies to every buyer who views the home.

When you close on the property, that commission is paid from the sale proceeds directly to the buyer's brokerage. Your agent never invoices you personally.

💡
Key pointThe buyer agent commission is built into the home price you pay. You do not write a cheque to your agent at closing — but the money still effectively comes from you through the purchase price.

Typical commission rates in 2026

There is no fixed commission rate in Ontario. Sellers decide what to offer and it varies by property type, market conditions, and individual seller preference.

Who actually pays the buyer's agent?

Technically the seller pays — it comes out of their proceeds at closing. But practically, it comes from the purchase price you agreed to pay. A home priced at $1M with a $25,000 buyer agent commission baked in would likely have been listed at $975,000 if no commission was offered.

This is why some buyers now negotiate directly or use cashback realtors — the commission was always part of the price they were paying.

2024 rule changes — what changed for buyers

In 2024, CREA and TRREB updated their rules to increase transparency around buyer agent compensation. The key changes:

  • Buyer agents must now disclose their compensation in writing before showing homes
  • Sellers are no longer required to offer buyer agent commission — though most still do
  • Buyers must sign a buyer representation agreement before their agent can show them properties
  • The agreement must specify how the agent will be compensated

These changes gave buyers more transparency — but did not reduce what agents typically earn. The same commission structures remain in place.

How to get the buyer agent commission back

Cashback realtors charge a flat fee and return the remaining buyer agent commission to you after closing. This is legal in Ontario under REBBA and must be disclosed in your buyer representation agreement.

Common questions

Do I need to pay my buyer's agent if the seller offers no commission? +
If the seller offers no buyer agent commission, your agent is not paid by the seller. Your buyer representation agreement must then specify how your agent will be compensated — either by you directly or through another arrangement. Always confirm commission before making an offer.
Can I negotiate the buyer agent commission down? +
You cannot change what the seller has offered on MLS. But you can choose an agent who charges less — like a flat-fee cashback realtor — who keeps only a portion and returns the rest to you. The effective result is the same as negotiating a lower commission.
Is cashback from a realtor taxable in Canada? +
Generally no. CRA considers cashback from a realtor to be a reduction in the purchase price of the home, not income. It is not reported as income and does not affect your taxes. Consult a tax advisor for your specific situation.
What happens to the buyer agent commission on new builds? +
New construction is different. Developers sometimes offer no buyer agent commission, or pay it separately outside of the purchase price. Always confirm with the builder and your agent before signing a purchase agreement.
