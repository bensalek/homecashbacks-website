How to Choose a Cashback Realtor in Toronto

Hidden fees, rotating agents, seller-side conflicts, and the questions most buyers never think to ask. Before you sign anything.

Not all cashback realtors are the same. The model varies significantly -- flat fees with hidden add-ons, rotating agents who don't know your situation, and some operations that aren't really representing you at all. A buyer who doesn't know what to look for can end up with less cashback, less service, or both.

This guide gives you the exact questions to ask and the specific things to verify before you commit.

Step 1

Verify they are RECO-registered

Any realtor offering cashback in Ontario must be registered with the Real Estate Council of Ontario (RECO) and work through a licensed brokerage. An unregistered person offering cashback is operating illegally.

Check registration directly at the RECO agent lookup: reco.on.ca → Find a Registered Salesperson. Takes 30 seconds. If they cannot provide a registration number, stop there.

Ask for their RECO registration number Verify it directly at reco.on.ca before your first showing
Confirm the brokerage they work under The cashback must be disclosed and processed through the brokerage
Confirm they are a member of TRREB Toronto Regional Real Estate Board membership gives them MLS access for Toronto listings
Step 2

Understand exactly how their fee works

There are two main cashback models. The difference in how much you actually get back is significant:

Model How it works On a $1M home
Flat fee Fixed amount regardless of price. You keep everything above that. $4,999 fee → $20,001 back
Percentage split Realtor keeps a % of commission. Their cut grows with the price. 1% kept → $15,000 back

A flat fee aligns the realtor's incentive with yours. They earn the same whether the home costs $600K or $1.5M -- so they focus on finding you the right home, not the most expensive one.

⚠ Watch for hidden fees inside "flat fee" models
Some cashback realtors advertise a flat fee but charge separately for:
  • Each showing (per-visit charges on top of the base fee)
  • Writing an offer (billed hourly or per offer)
  • Reviewing a condo status certificate
  • Any negotiation beyond the initial offer

Ask explicitly: "Does your fee cover showings, offer writing, negotiation, and closing coordination -- or are any of those billed separately?" Get the answer in writing.
Step 3

Will you have a dedicated realtor?

Some cashback operations run high volume -- they assign whoever is available to each showing. You might deal with a different agent every time. That agent doesn't know your situation, your budget, your timeline, or what you've already seen. They cannot negotiate effectively on your behalf.

Ask directly: "Will I work with the same realtor from my first showing through to closing?" A good cashback realtor will say yes without hesitation.

One dedicated realtor from first showing to closing Continuity matters -- your agent should know your full situation before writing any offer
Active involvement -- not just signing paperwork Your realtor should be at showings, reviewing listings, advising on offer strategy, and present at closing
Responsive communication Ask how quickly they typically respond and what the best way to reach them is
Step 4 -- Critical

The seller agent risk most buyers never hear about

Some cashback services are not real buyer representation. Instead of assigning you a buyer agent, they tell you to contact the listing agent directly to arrange showings.

⚠ This is a serious conflict of interest
The listing agent legally represents the seller. Their fiduciary duty is to get the seller the highest price and best terms -- not to help you. When you contact a listing agent directly as an unrepresented buyer:

  • You receive no independent advice on price, conditions, or offer strategy
  • Everything you share (your budget, your timeline, how much you want the home) goes to the seller's side
  • Some listing agents charge unrepresented buyers 1% to 1.5% for showing the property -- meaning you pay more and get no representation

Always have your own registered buyer agent who represents only you.

Ask any cashback realtor directly: "Will you personally attend showings with me, or am I expected to contact listing agents myself?" If the answer is anything other than yes to the first part, that is not a buyer representation service.

Step 5

Red flags to walk away from

!
Cashback promised verbally but not in writing If it is not in the buyer representation agreement before you make offers, it is not guaranteed.
!
Per-showing, per-offer, or hourly fees on top of the "flat" fee A genuine flat fee covers everything. Hidden add-ons can eliminate most of your cashback.
!
Directed to contact listing agents yourself This is not buyer representation. The listing agent works for the seller, not you.
!
No dedicated agent -- different people at every showing A rotating team cannot negotiate effectively because they don't know your full situation.
!
Cannot provide a RECO registration number Verify at reco.on.ca before any commitment. No exceptions.
!
Pressure to sign an exclusive agreement before any showings A first showing should be free with no commitment. Immediate exclusivity demands are a warning sign.
!
Cashback only applies if you find the home yourself This is not full service buyer representation. You lose negotiation support, offer strategy, and legal protection.
