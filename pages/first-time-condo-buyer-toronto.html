First Time Condo Buyer Toronto

The incentives are the same as any first home. What's different is the due diligence. Here's exactly what changes when your first home is a condo.

The short answer

The incentives don't change, the checklist does

Every first-time buyer incentive in Ontario applies exactly the same way to a condo as it does to a detached house. What actually changes for a first-time condo buyer is the due diligence: a status certificate to review, a reserve fund to check, monthly fees to budget for, and a condo corporation with its own rules to understand, none of which a first-time house buyer has to think about.

Down payment rules

How much down payment do you actually need

Minimum down payment in Canada is tiered by price, and it applies the same way whether you're buying a condo or a house. Most first-time condo purchases in Toronto fall entirely in the lowest tier, given current condo pricing.

Purchase price Minimum down payment
Up to $500,000 5% of the purchase price
$500,000 to $1,499,999 5% on the first $500,000, 10% on the remainder
$1,500,000 and above 20% of the purchase price

Anything below 20% down requires CMHC mortgage insurance, which adds a premium to your mortgage principal.

What's actually different

The condo-specific checklist a first-time buyer needs

A repeat condo buyer knows to check these automatically. A first-time buyer often doesn't know they exist until something goes wrong.

Review the status certificate before you go firm Covers financials, the reserve fund, and building rules. Your lawyer should review it, not just you.
Budget for monthly condo fees as a real, ongoing cost Fees are not optional or negotiable after closing. Factor them into your monthly budget the same as your mortgage.
Check the reserve fund, not just the fee amount A low fee with an underfunded reserve often means a special assessment is coming.
Understand what your fee actually includes Heat, water, and amenities are sometimes included, sometimes not. Get the exact breakdown in writing.
Read the building's rules before you commit Rentals, pets, and renovations are governed by the condo corporation, not just by you.
