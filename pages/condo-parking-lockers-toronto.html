Condo Parking & Lockers Explained

Not always included, not always what you think you're getting. Here's what to actually confirm before you assume.

The short answer

Don't assume parking comes with the unit

Especially in downtown Toronto, parking is frequently sold or leased separately from the condo unit itself. A listing that doesn't explicitly say parking is included probably doesn't have it bundled in. Always confirm in writing exactly what's included in the purchase price before you make assumptions based on the listing photos.

The legal structure, in plain terms

You don't own the spot the way you own the unit

A parking spot or locker is typically what's called an exclusive-use common element. It's technically part of the condominium's shared property, but you have the exclusive legal right to use it. This is different from a freehold garage or driveway that you own outright, and it affects how the spot can be transferred, sold, or included in your fees.

Check the declaration for transfer rulesSome buildings allow parking and lockers to be sold separately to another owner in the same building, others require them to stay bundled with a specific unit.
Confirm the spot number and unit number match in the APSGet the specific spot and locker numbers documented, not just a general statement that one is included.
Ask if maintenance cost is separate or bundledSince it's part of the common elements, its share of upkeep is usually already reflected in your overall fee, not billed separately.
The trend worth knowing about

EV charging is starting to affect resale value

Demand for EV-ready parking has grown quickly, and it's genuinely outpacing supply in many older Toronto buildings. Condo boards are increasingly retrofitting garages to add charging capacity as more residents ask for it.

A real Toronto retrofit example
A midtown Toronto condo installed chargers across57 parking spots
Share of the building's units with charging accessOver 20%
Cited potential resale premium from EV-ready parkingUp to 5%

Retrofit costs vary enormously depending on the building's existing electrical capacity. Some buildings have achieved installs for a few hundred dollars per spot using modern load-sharing systems, while others face much higher costs if the building's electrical infrastructure needs a bigger upgrade first. If EV charging matters to you, ask directly whether the building has it or has any plans to add it, rather than assuming.
