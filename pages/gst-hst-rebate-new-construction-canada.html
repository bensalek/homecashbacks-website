GST/HST Rebate on New Construction

Not every builder pays this rebate the same way, and it changes how much cash and financing you actually need at closing. Here's exactly how it works, and what your lender will and won't count.

The short answer

There are two completely different paths, and it depends on your contract

This is the single most common source of confusion with the new rebate. Some builders credit it directly, quietly lowering what you owe at closing. Others don't, and expect you to pay the full amount, then chase the rebate from CRA yourself afterward. Both are legitimate, both are common, and which one applies to you comes down entirely to how your Agreement of Purchase and Sale is worded, not anything you can assume.

What decides which path you get
Whether your APS requires you to assign the rebate to the builder
Whether your agreement was signed before the rules were finalized
Whether the agreement addresses the new rebate at all
The specific builder's own internal policy
Path one

The builder credits it, and you simply pay less

This is the simplest outcome, and it's how most builders are now structuring new agreements. You and the builder jointly complete Form GST190 (the GST/HST New Housing Rebate Application), you assign your rebate rights to the builder, and the builder reduces the amount you owe at closing by the rebate amount. The builder then claims the rebate from CRA on their own. You never file a separate claim, and you never see a cheque, you just close on a lower number.

Even here, don't assume your lender treats it the same way
A builder crediting the rebate doesn't automatically mean your lender approves your mortgage against that lower number. Builders are often reluctant to reduce the actual contract price, since that price sets the appraisal comparable for every other unit in the development, so rebates and incentives get structured as credits rather than a straightforward price cut. Some lenders and appraisers look past that structuring and still underwrite against the original, pre-rebate contract price. Confirm with your mortgage broker specifically which number your lender is qualifying you against, the discounted price or the original one, before you assume your financing need matches your discounted closing cost.
Path two

The builder doesn't credit it, and you claim it yourself after closing

Many builders are taking this position, particularly on agreements that were signed before the rules were finalized, or where the original contract simply doesn't address the new rebate. In this case, you pay the full purchase price, including the full HST, at closing. You then apply directly to CRA yourself, generally within a two-year window from taking possession or substantial completion, and the rebate arrives as a separate payment afterward.

The real-world impact of this path
This isn't just a paperwork inconvenience. It means you need financing and cash on hand for the full, non-discounted price at closing, even though you're entitled to thousands of dollars back. The rebate shows up weeks or months later, completely separate from your mortgage. Budget for the full amount, not the rebate-adjusted amount, unless your builder has confirmed in writing that they're crediting it directly.
The part almost nobody explains

How lenders actually treat this rebate

Here's the piece that catches buyers off guard even when they understand the two paths above. Because the rebate isn't guaranteed cash in hand at the time your mortgage is underwritten, most lenders will not treat it as an asset for down payment or closing cost purposes, even if your builder has agreed to credit it. Down payment and closing funds generally need to be verified and seasoned in your account for 30 to 90 days. A rebate you haven't actually received yet simply doesn't meet that bar.

Scenario What this means for financing
Builder credits the rebate Your effective purchase price is lower, so the mortgage amount you need to qualify for is lower too. Straightforward.
Builder doesn't credit it, you claim from CRA You need to qualify for, and have available, financing and cash for the full, non-discounted price. The rebate is not counted toward your closing funds.

This is exactly why confirming which path applies to your specific purchase, in writing, before you finalize your financing plan, matters as much as the rebate amount itself.

Before you sign or finance anything

What to actually check

Read the GST/HST section of your Agreement of Purchase and Sale Look specifically for language about rebate assignment, not just a general mention of HST.
Get the builder's position in writing If the agreement is silent or unclear, ask directly and get a written answer before you rely on it.
Ask your lender directly which price they're underwriting against A builder credit doesn't guarantee your lender approves you against the discounted number. Get this confirmed in writing, not assumed.
Tell your mortgage broker which path applies Your pre-approval and closing funds plan need to assume the full price unless the credit is confirmed.
Confirm your eligibility for the first-time buyer version The larger federal rebate, up to $50,000, requires you to be a genuine first-time buyer under CRA's rules.

Rules on both programs have changed multiple times since being introduced, confirm the current figures with your accountant before relying on them.
