What Is a Flat Fee Realtor in Toronto?

A flat fee realtor charges one fixed price to sell your home, not a percentage of it. Here's what that actually means, how it differs from a discount broker, and what to check before you choose one.

The short answer

A fixed price instead of a percentage

A flat fee realtor is a fully licensed REALTOR® who charges a fixed dollar amount to list and sell your home, instead of the standard percentage commission. In Toronto, flat fees generally run from about $4,999 to $6,000 or more depending on the brokerage, compared to a standard 2.5% commission, which is $25,000 on a $1,000,000 home. The fee structure is a business decision, not a difference in licensing or legal obligation to you as a client.

What "flat fee" should mean, at minimum
Same MLS access as a percentage-commission listing
Professional photography included, not extra
A dedicated agent, not a call centre
Full negotiation and closing support

Because the fee is fixed, the savings grow the more your home is worth. This is the flat fee model's core appeal: the cost of the work doesn't scale with your home's price, so there's no reason your fee should either.

The distinction that matters most

Flat fee is not the same as bare-bones discount

This is where flat fee services genuinely differ from each other, and it's the single most important thing to check before you sign anything. Some services marketed as flat fee or discount cut what's included to make the lower price work. A genuine flat fee realtor delivers the same full service as a percentage-commission agent, just priced differently, not a stripped-down version of one.

Before you sign

Questions to ask any flat fee realtor

Is the fee genuinely flat, with no hidden add-ons? Get the complete list of what's included, and what costs extra if anything, in writing.
Is the realtor registered with RECO? Verify directly at registrantsearch.reco.on.ca before you commit to anything.
Who negotiates my offers, and are they the same person throughout? A single dedicated agent who knows your file negotiates more effectively than a rotating team.
When is the fee actually paid? A genuine flat fee model should be paid at closing, from the sale proceeds, not upfront.
