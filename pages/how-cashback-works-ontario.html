How the buyer agent commission flows in Ontario

When a seller lists a home on the MLS in Ontario, they specify a buyer agent commission upfront — before any offer is made. This is a fixed amount, typically 2 to 2.5% of the sale price, that goes to whoever represents the buyer at closing.

This commission is built into the listing price. It flows from the seller's proceeds at closing, through the selling brokerage, to the buyer's brokerage, and then to the buyer's agent. The buyer never writes a cheque for it directly — but the money effectively comes from the purchase price they agreed to pay.

What a cashback realtor actually does

A cashback realtor is a licensed real estate agent or brokerage that charges less than the full buyer agent commission and returns the difference to the buyer after closing. They provide the same core services as a traditional buyer's agent — showings, offer writing, negotiation, closing coordination — but with a different fee structure.

The cashback is not a discount on services. It is a rebate from the commission that was always going to be paid. The realtor simply takes less of it.

What you still get with a cashback realtor

  • Full MLS access and property search
  • Showings across your target area
  • Comparative market analysis before offers
  • Offer writing and negotiation
  • Condition review and due diligence guidance
  • Closing coordination from accepted offer to keys
  • Full brokerage legal protections under RECO
⚠️
What varies between cashback realtorsThe level of service, availability, and communication quality varies. A lower fee does not guarantee lower service — but it does not guarantee equal service either. Ask about response times, showing availability, and how many active clients they work with.

The two cashback models — flat fee vs percentage

Ontario cashback realtors use one of two fee structures. Understanding the difference matters — the math is very different depending on your home price.

Flat fee model

The realtor charges a fixed dollar amount regardless of the home price and returns everything above that to the buyer. On higher-priced homes, buyers receive significantly more cashback.

Percentage model

The realtor keeps a percentage of the commission (typically 50%) and returns the other 50% to the buyer. The realtor earns more on more expensive homes.

Is it legal in Ontario?

Yes. Cashback from a buyer's agent is fully legal in Ontario. It is permitted under the Trust in Real Estate Services Act (TRESA, 2002), formerly REBBA, and regulated by the Real Estate Council of Ontario (RECO).

The legal requirements are:

  • The cashback amount must be disclosed in the buyer representation agreement before any offer is made
  • The agreement must be signed by both the buyer and the brokerage
  • The cashback must flow through the brokerage — not directly from agent to buyer outside the transaction
  • All commissions and rebates must be disclosed to all parties in the transaction
✓
Bottom lineIf the cashback is written into your buyer representation agreement before you make any offers, it is legal, binding, and protected under Ontario real estate law. A verbal promise of cashback is not enforceable — always get it in writing.

What changed in 2024 for Ontario buyers

In 2024, CREA and provincial regulators updated the rules around buyer agent compensation in Ontario. The key changes:

  • Buyers must sign a buyer representation agreement before any agent can show them a property
  • Agents must disclose their full compensation in writing before any showing
  • Sellers are no longer required to offer buyer agent commission — though most still do
  • If the seller's commission is less than the agent's agreed fee, the buyer may be responsible for the shortfall

For cashback buyers, the 2024 changes are largely positive. The requirement to disclose compensation in writing means the cashback amount must be formally documented — making it legally enforceable rather than relying on an agent's goodwill.

What to look for before signing with a cashback realtor

Fee structure in writing

The cashback amount or formula must be in your buyer representation agreement before you sign. "We'll work out the cashback at closing" is not acceptable — and not enforceable.

RECO registration

Verify the agent and brokerage are registered with RECO. You can check at reco.on.ca. An unregistered individual offering cashback is operating illegally and you have no legal protection.

Full service scope

Confirm what is and is not included. Some cashback models limit the number of showings, charge per offer, or exclude certain property types. Understand the full scope before committing.

Holdover clause

Check how long the holdover clause lasts. If you cancel the agreement and then buy a property you were shown during the term, you may still owe commission. A reasonable holdover is 30 to 60 days. Anything longer is worth negotiating.

What happens if the seller offers less commission

Ask directly: if the seller's commission is less than your fee, who pays the difference? A transparent cashback realtor will answer this clearly before you sign anything.

Risks and limitations

Cashback realtors are not the right fit for every buyer. A few things to consider:

Some sellers may react negatively

In some competitive situations, listing agents may privately prefer buyers represented by traditional agents. This is not legal discrimination, but it can affect how your offer is received. A strong cashback agent will manage this professionally.

Service levels vary significantly

The cashback model does not guarantee quality. Some cashback realtors run high volumes and provide minimal personal attention. Ask how many active buyer clients they currently work with and how quickly they respond to showing requests.

Pre-construction may be different

New construction developers sometimes offer no buyer agent commission, or structure compensation differently. Confirm the commission terms before any pre-construction agreement — cashback may not apply in the same way.

Low-priced homes may not benefit much

On homes under $400,000, the buyer agent commission may not be much larger than a flat fee. Run the math before assuming cashback will be significant at lower price points.

Common questions

Can any Ontario realtor offer cashback? +
Yes, any RECO-registered agent or brokerage can offer cashback. There is no special licence required. The only requirement is that the cashback be properly disclosed in the buyer representation agreement and processed through the brokerage. Individual agents cannot pay cashback directly outside the brokerage structure.
Does cashback affect my mortgage approval? +
Generally no — the cashback is received after closing, not at the time of purchase. It does not affect your mortgage qualification or down payment calculation. Some lenders may want to know about it if it is unusually large, but for most buyers it has no impact on financing. Ask your mortgage broker to confirm for your specific situation.
Why don't most Ontario realtors offer cashback? +
Because the traditional commission model pays very well. A buyer's agent on a $1M Toronto home earns $20,000 to $25,000 for the same work a cashback agent does for $4,999 to $12,500. Most agents and brokerages have no financial incentive to change a system that works in their favour. The buyers who benefit most from cashback are the ones who understand the commission structure and actively look for a different model.
Is cashback the same as a commission rebate? +
Yes — the terms are used interchangeably. Whether it is called a cashback, rebate, or commission rebate, the mechanics are the same: the buyer's agent receives the commission from the seller's brokerage at closing, keeps their fee, and returns the remainder to the buyer.
Can I negotiate cashback with my current agent? +
You can ask — but most traditional agents will decline. Their brokerage structures, split agreements, and business models are built around keeping the full commission. You are more likely to get a meaningful cashback by working with an agent or brokerage whose model is specifically built around it from the start.
