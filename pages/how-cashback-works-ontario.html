How Cashback Realtors Work in Ontario

The buyer agent commission exists in every deal. Cashback realtors take less and return the rest to you.

Where the money comes from

When a seller lists on MLS, they specify a buyer agent commission upfront — typically 2 to 2.5% of the sale price. This money goes to whoever represents the buyer at closing. It exists in every deal, regardless of which agent you choose.

Flat fee vs percentage — which returns more

Ontario cashback realtors use one of two structures. The math is very different depending on your home price.

For GTA buyers above $700K, the flat fee model returns significantly more. The higher the price, the bigger the gap.

Is it legal in Ontario?

Yes. Cashback is fully legal under the Trust in Real Estate Services Act (TRESA) and regulated by RECO. Three requirements:

  • The cashback amount must be written into your buyer representation agreement before any offer
  • It must flow through the brokerage — not directly from agent to buyer
  • All commissions and rebates must be disclosed to all parties
✓
Bottom lineWritten into your agreement before offers = legal and binding. A verbal promise of cashback is not enforceable.

Since 2024, agents must disclose their full compensation in writing before any showing. This actually helps cashback buyers — the amount is now a legal obligation, not a goodwill gesture. One new risk: if the seller's commission is less than your agent's minimum fee, you may owe the difference. A good cashback agent flags this before you proceed.

Common questions

Does cashback affect my mortgage approval? +
Generally no. Cashback is received after closing, so it does not affect your mortgage qualification or down payment. Ask your mortgage broker to confirm for your specific situation.
Is cashback taxable in Canada? +
Generally no. CRA treats it as a reduction in the purchase price of the home, not income. Consult a tax advisor for investment property purchases.
Why don't most Ontario realtors offer cashback? +
Because the traditional model pays well. A buyer's agent on a $1M home earns $20,000 to $25,000. Most agents have no financial incentive to change a system that works in their favour.
Does pre-construction work the same way? +
Not always. New construction developers sometimes offer no buyer agent commission or structure it differently. Confirm commission terms before any pre-construction agreement.
Can I negotiate cashback with my current agent? +
You can ask — but most traditional agents will decline. Their brokerage structures are built around keeping the full commission. You are more likely to get meaningful cashback by working with an agent whose model is built around it from the start.
