Downsizing to a Condo

Selling a house and buying a condo at the same time is really two transactions that need to work together. Here's how to sequence it without a gap in housing.

The real challenge

This is two deals that need to be coordinated, not one

Downsizing means managing a sale and a purchase together, and the sequencing matters as much as either transaction individually. Getting the timing wrong can mean paying for temporary housing, or carrying two properties at once. Getting it right means one seamless move.

The core decision

Sell first, or buy first

Sell firstBuy first
Budget certaintyYou know exactly what you have to spendYou're estimating based on an unsold house
Housing gap riskReal risk of needing temporary housingAvoided, if closings align
Offer strength on your condoStronger, no conditions needed on your saleWeaker if made conditional on your house selling
Carrying costsNone, one property at a timePossible, if you own both briefly

There's no universally correct answer. Selling first is generally lower financial risk but higher logistical risk. Buying first is the reverse. Your comfort with each type of risk, and how confident you are in your house selling quickly, should drive the decision.

Bridging the gap

If your closings don't line up

If you're using proceeds from your house sale toward your condo purchase but the closing dates don't align, a bridge loan can cover the gap, letting your lender advance funds against your house sale before it actually closes. Talk to your mortgage broker early about this option if your timeline looks tight, it's much easier to arrange proactively than to scramble for at the last minute.

What actually changes

Life in a condo after years in a house

One predictable monthly fee instead of unpredictable repair costsNo more surprise roof or furnace bills, but budget realistically for the fee itself and occasional special assessments.
No more yard work or snow removalGenuinely one of the most appreciated changes for downsizers, whether or not it was the main motivation.
Downsizing your belongings, not just your square footageGive yourself real time for this, it's often the most emotionally involved part of the move, more than the transactions themselves.
Freed-up equity to plan forA lower purchase price than your house sale often means real proceeds left over. Decide what that's for before it lands in your account.
