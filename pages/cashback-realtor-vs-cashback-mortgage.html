Cashback Realtor vs. Cash-Back Mortgage

Two terms that sound identical and work nothing alike. Here's the real difference, and whether you can use both.

By Laleh Mousavi, Licensed Realtor · RECO · Last updated: September 2026 · 5 min read
Cashback realtor Cash-back mortgage Side by side Can you combine them? Questions

What is a cashback realtor?

When you buy a home, the seller has already agreed to pay a buyer agent commission, typically 2 to 2.5% of the purchase price. That money goes to whichever agent represents you. A cashback realtor keeps a smaller, flat portion of that commission and returns the rest to you after closing.

💡
The key pointThis money was never the lender's or the bank's. It comes entirely from the commission the seller already agreed to pay. Nothing about your mortgage changes.

It costs you nothing to receive it, doesn't require you to borrow more, and doesn't touch your interest rate. See our full guide on how cashback realtors work in Ontario for the complete breakdown.

What is a cash-back mortgage?

A cash-back mortgage is a completely different product, offered by your lender, not your realtor. You borrow more than you need to close, and the lender pays you the difference as a lump sum, usually 1% to 7% of your mortgage amount.

The catch is built into the rate. Cash-back mortgages carry a meaningfully higher interest rate than a standard mortgage of the same term, and that higher rate applies to your entire loan balance, not just the cash-back portion. Over a full term, the extra interest you pay often comes close to, or exceeds, the cash you received upfront.

What happensDetail
Lump sum1% to 7% of your mortgage amount, deposited around closing
The tradeoffA higher interest rate on your full mortgage balance for the whole term
Mortgage qualificationThe higher rate raises your stress-test rate, which can reduce how much you qualify to borrow
Breaking the mortgage earlyYou typically owe back a pro-rated portion of the cash, plus your regular prepayment penalty

None of this makes a cash-back mortgage a bad option outright. For someone who is certain they'll hold their mortgage for the full term and needs cash for closing costs or debt, the math can work out. But it's a loan with real conditions attached, not a rebate.

Can you use both?

Yes. Because they come from two entirely separate parties, one from the brokerage representing you and one from the bank lending you money, choosing one has no bearing on your eligibility for the other. There's no rule preventing a buyer from working with a cashback realtor and also selecting a cash-back mortgage product from their lender.

That said, they're not equivalent, and stacking them doesn't mean stacking free money. The realtor cashback genuinely costs you nothing. The mortgage cash-back is money you're borrowing at a premium rate, so it's worth evaluating on its own terms with a mortgage broker, separate from the decision to work with a cashback realtor.

Common questions

Is a cashback realtor the same as a cash-back mortgage? +
No. A cashback realtor returns part of their commission to you after closing, with no cost attached. A cash-back mortgage is a loan from your lender paid upfront, in exchange for a higher interest rate on your entire mortgage.
Can I use both a cashback realtor and a cash-back mortgage? +
Yes. They come from two unrelated parties, your realtor and your lender, so choosing one does not affect your eligibility for the other.
Does realtor cashback affect my mortgage interest rate? +
No. Realtor cashback is paid after closing and has no connection to your mortgage terms. It does not raise your rate or affect your qualification.
What happens if I break a cash-back mortgage early? +
Most lenders require you to repay a pro-rated portion of the cash you received, on top of the regular prepayment penalty. This can significantly reduce or eliminate the benefit if you sell, refinance, or switch lenders before your term ends.
