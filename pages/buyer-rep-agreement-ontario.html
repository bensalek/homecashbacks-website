What is a buyer representation agreement?

A buyer representation agreement (BRA) is a contract between you and a real estate brokerage. It authorizes the brokerage — through a specific agent — to represent your interests in a home purchase. Without it, the agent legally cannot act on your behalf.

The agreement sets out the agent's duties to you, the area and property type you are searching for, the time period of the agreement, and how the agent will be compensated.

📋
Plain language versionIt is essentially a contract that says: "This agent works for me, not for the seller." It gives you legal protections and gives the agent the right to be paid.

What changed in 2024

Before 2024, buyers could view properties with an agent without signing any agreement. Many buyers worked informally with agents for months before committing.

In 2024, CREA and provincial regulators updated the rules. The key changes in Ontario:

  • Agents must have a signed buyer representation agreement before showing any property
  • The agreement must disclose how the agent will be paid — including if they receive a buyer agent commission from the seller
  • If the seller's commission does not cover the agent's agreed fee, the buyer may be responsible for the difference
  • Buyers cannot be shown homes "informally" — every showing requires a signed agreement
⚠️
What this means practicallyYou will be asked to sign a BRA before your first showing. This is now legally required, not just a formality. Read it before you sign.

What to watch for before signing

Long holdover clauses

A holdover clause means if you buy a property that you were shown during the agreement period — even after the agreement expires — the agent is still entitled to their commission. A holdover of 60 to 90 days is standard. Longer than that, push back.

Vague compensation terms

The agreement must specify exactly how your agent is paid. If it says "as offered by the seller" without specifying a minimum, you could end up in a situation where the seller offers less than expected and you owe the difference.

Wide geographic scope

If the agreement covers "all of Ontario" and you later decide to buy in a different city with a different agent, you could technically still owe commission to the first agent. Limit the scope to the specific cities and neighbourhoods you are actually searching.

Dual representation

If your agent also represents the seller of a home you want to buy, they are in a conflict of interest. They cannot fully advocate for either party. In Ontario this requires written consent from both parties — but the better move is to find an agent who represents buyers only.

Can you cancel a buyer representation agreement?

Yes — but the terms depend on what you signed. Most agreements can be cancelled by mutual consent. The agent signs a form acknowledging the cancellation and releasing you from the agreement.

The holdover clause, however, survives cancellation. If you cancel and then buy a property you were previously shown, you may still owe commission.

  • Ask for a cancellation clause — the right to cancel with written notice
  • Negotiate the holdover period to 30 days or less
  • Get any cancellation in writing — a verbal agreement is not enforceable

How cashback is written into the agreement

If you work with a cashback realtor, the cashback amount must be specified in your buyer representation agreement. This is a legal requirement under RECO rules — it cannot be a verbal promise.

A properly written cashback clause will specify:

  • The flat fee the agent retains (e.g. $4,999)
  • How the remaining commission is calculated
  • When you receive the cashback (typically within days of closing)
  • What happens if the seller's commission is less than $4,999

Common questions

Can I work with multiple agents at the same time in Ontario? +
Not if you have a signed buyer representation agreement with one of them. The agreement is typically exclusive — meaning you cannot have another agent represent you in the same area for the same type of property during the same period. Once the agreement expires or is cancelled, you are free to work with anyone.
What if the seller does not offer enough commission to cover my agent's fee? +
Under the 2024 rules, if the seller's offered commission is less than what your agent's agreement specifies, you may be responsible for the shortfall. This is a new risk for buyers and a key reason to read your agreement carefully. With HomeCashbacks, our flat $4,999 fee means this situation is clearly defined upfront.
Does signing a BRA mean I have to buy? +
No. A buyer representation agreement does not obligate you to purchase any property. It only obligates you to work exclusively with that agent during the term — and to pay their commission if you do buy a property through them or that they showed you during the holdover period.
