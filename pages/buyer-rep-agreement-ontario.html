What is a buyer representation agreement?

A buyer representation agreement (BRA) is a contract between you and a real estate brokerage. It authorizes a specific agent to represent your interests in a home purchase. Without it, the agent cannot legally act on your behalf.

📋
Plain languageIt says: "This agent works for me, not the seller." It gives you legal protections. It gives the agent the right to be paid.

Since 2024, you must sign a BRA before your first showing. Agents must also disclose their full compensation in writing — including any cashback — before showing you any property.

What to watch for before signing

  • Long holdover clause — 60 to 90 days is standard. Anything longer, push back. If you cancel and buy a property you were shown, you may still owe commission during this window.
  • Vague compensation — The agreement must state exactly how your agent is paid. "As offered by the seller" without a minimum leaves you exposed if the seller offers less than expected.
  • Wide geographic scope — If it covers "all of Ontario" and you later use a different agent in another city, you could technically still owe the first agent. Limit the scope.
  • Dual representation — If your agent also represents the seller, they cannot fully advocate for you. In Ontario this requires written consent — the better move is to simply avoid it.

Can you cancel?

Yes, by mutual consent. The agent signs a form releasing you from the agreement. Three things to know:

  • Ask for a cancellation clause before signing — the right to cancel with written notice
  • The holdover clause survives cancellation — buying a previously-shown property still triggers commission
  • Get any cancellation in writing — verbal agreements are not enforceable

How cashback is written into the agreement

If you work with a cashback realtor, the cashback amount must be in your BRA. This is a legal requirement — a verbal promise is not enforceable.

A properly written cashback clause specifies:

  • The flat fee the agent retains (e.g. $4,999)
  • How the remaining commission is calculated
  • When you receive the cashback (typically within days of closing)
  • What happens if the seller's commission is less than $4,999

Common questions

Can I work with multiple agents at the same time? +
Not if you have a signed agreement with one of them. The BRA is typically exclusive for the same area and property type during the term. Once it expires or is cancelled, you are free to work with anyone.
What if the seller does not offer enough commission? +
Under the 2024 rules, if the seller's commission is less than your agent's agreed fee, you may be responsible for the shortfall. This is a key reason to read your agreement carefully and confirm commission terms before making any offer.
Does signing a BRA mean I have to buy? +
No. It does not obligate you to purchase any property. It only requires you to work exclusively with that agent during the term — and to pay their commission if you buy a property through them or that they showed you during the holdover period.
