The part most investors get wrong

Which rebates actually apply to an investment purchase

Most first-time buyer incentives are built around primary residence, and investors don't qualify for them. But there is a genuine, current rebate that does apply, and most investors don't know about it yet.

Program Applies to investors?
Ontario & Toronto Land Transfer Tax Rebates No, primary residence and first-time buyer only
Federal First-Time Home Buyers' GST Rebate No, primary residence and first-time buyer only
New Residential Rental Property Rebate (NRRPR) Yes, on new construction with a 1+ year lease

The NRRPR is currently enhanced under the same temporary window as the owner-occupied HST rebate, agreements signed between April 1, 2026 and March 31, 2027 can access a combined federal and provincial rebate of up to $130,000, the same cap as the primary residence version, and it applies to genuine long-term rental use, not first-time buyers specifically.

The cash flow catch investors need to plan for
Unlike an owner-occupied purchase, where the builder can often credit the rebate directly, investment purchasers commonly have to pay the full HST at closing and claim the rebate from CRA afterward, once a qualifying long-term lease is signed. That means you need financing and cash for the full price upfront, with the rebate arriving separately, later. See our full breakdown of how builders and lenders handle this.
A common, costly assumption

You generally can't Airbnb a pure investment property in Toronto

This trips up new investors more than almost anything else. Toronto's short-term rental bylaw restricts stays under 28 consecutive days to the host's principal residence only. A property you don't actually live in cannot legally be operated as a short-term rental in the city, no matter what the condo corporation allows.

Registration required, and it's tied to one principal residence 2026 fee is $375 to register, $390 to renew annually. You cannot register multiple investment units.
Fines run from $1,000 up to $100,000 Non-compliant listings are also actively removed from platforms, and daily fines apply for continued violations.
Condo corporations can ban it independently Even where city rules would allow it, many downtown Toronto condo declarations prohibit short-term rentals outright.
This is exactly why the NRRPR requires a long-term lease The rebate program and the bylaw both push in the same direction: long-term rental, not short-term.

If short-term rental income is part of your investment thesis, plan around your own principal residence, or a jurisdiction outside Toronto with different rules, not a Toronto investment condo.

Before you buy a condo as an investment

What to check, specifically as an investor

Beyond the general condo due diligence every buyer should do, investors have a few checks that matter more.

Read the declaration's rental restriction clause Some buildings cap the percentage of units that can be rented out at all, not just short-term.
Check the current rental cap isn't already full If a building has a rental cap and it's already at the limit, you may not be able to rent your unit out at all, even long-term.
Pull real comparable rents for the unit type and building Listing prices are not achieved rents. Ask for what similar units in the same building actually leased for recently.
Review our full condo red flags and questions to ask Reserve fund health and litigation history matter even more when your return depends on the building staying well run.
Tax treatment

Is cashback taxable on an investment property?

The CRA generally treats a commission rebate as a reduction in your purchase price, not taxable income, for a primary residence. Where a property is bought as an investment, or through a corporation, the treatment can differ. Speak with your accountant about your specific situation before assuming either way.
