Pre-Construction Condo Cashback Toronto

A temporary HST rebate window is live right now, worth up to $130,000 on qualifying new homes. Here's how it's actually calculated, and how cashback works on a pre-construction purchase.

The current window

A temporary HST rebate, larger than most buyers realize

For purchase agreements signed between April 1, 2026 and March 31, 2027, Ontario's temporary enhanced new housing rebate combines with the federal rebate to remove up to $130,000 in HST on qualifying new homes priced up to $1,500,000, stepping down on a sliding scale up to $1,850,000. This one is not limited to first-time buyers. Outside this window, the standard combined rebate is typically closer to $24,000 to $30,000.

The current rebate, at a glance
Up to $130,000 combined, homes up to $1,500,000
Not limited to first-time buyers

Rules like this are complex and change with each budget. Confirm the exact current numbers with your accountant or lawyer before you rely on them in an offer.

How it actually works

How builders calculate and apply the rebate

This is the part most buyers don't realize until closing. Builders typically advertise a price that already has the standard rebate built in, known as the Stated Price Net of Rebates. You don't apply for the rebate yourself and you don't get a cheque in the mail. At closing, you sign a document assigning your rebate rights to the builder, and the builder credits the rebate directly against your closing costs. The saving shows up as a lower amount owing on your final statement, not as money sent to you.

Not every builder handles it this way. Some require you to pay the full amount and claim the rebate from CRA yourself afterward, which also changes how much financing you need to qualify for. See our full breakdown of both paths and how lenders treat each one.

The eligibility condition that catches buyers off guard
The rebate generally requires you or an immediate family member to use the unit as your primary residence, typically for at least one year after closing. If you sell or rent it out instead without qualifying under the separate rental rebate program, the builder can reclaim the rebate amount from you, sometimes with interest. If you're buying to rent out, ask about the New Residential Rental Property Rebate instead, a different program with its own rules.
Real protections you already have

Three buyer protections worth knowing about

A 10-day cooling-off period, no reason required You can cancel and get your full deposit back within 10 days of receiving the signed agreement, the disclosure statement, and the Condo Buyers' Guide, for any reason at all.
Your deposit is protected up to $20,000 For a condo purchase specifically, deposits are protected up to $20,000 under the Ontario New Home Warranties Plan, with amounts above that protected under the Condominium Act. This is separate from the deposit protection tiers on freehold new construction.
Compensation if occupancy is seriously delayed If the builder misses the Firm Occupancy Date set out in your Tarion Addendum, you're entitled to compensation of $150 per day for living expenses, up to a maximum of $7,500.
Before you visit a sales office

Not every builder works with buyer agents

This is worth knowing before you get attached to a specific building. Builder policies vary, some welcome buyer agents and pay a commission the same way a resale seller does, others don't accept agent representation for certain projects or phases at all. Always confirm directly with the specific builder before assuming representation is available.

Register before your first visit, not after
When a builder does work with agents, you generally need to be registered with, or accompanied by, your agent at your very first contact with the sales office. Walk in alone first, and most builders will not retroactively pay a commission to an agent you bring in later, which also means no cashback on that purchase.
How cashback applies

Cashback on a pre-construction purchase

Where a builder does work with buyer agents, the mechanics are the same as a resale purchase. The builder pays the buyer's agent commission directly, you pay nothing extra either way. We take a flat $4,999 from that commission and return the rest to you after closing.
