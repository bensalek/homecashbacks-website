Low Commission Realtor Toronto

Standard commission in Toronto runs 2.5% to 5%. A low commission realtor charges a flat fee instead, whether you're buying or selling. Here's the real math, and what to check before you sign.

The short answer

What does "low commission realtor" actually mean?

A low commission realtor charges a flat fee or reduced percentage instead of the standard commission structure. In Toronto, that standard is typically 2.5% per side, meaning 5% total on a home sale. A low commission model, like a flat $4,999 fee, saves a seller real money on the way out, and lets a buyer keep most of the commission the seller already set aside, as cashback.

Two ways "low commission" applies
Selling: flat fee instead of 2.5% listing commission
Buying: cashback on the commission the seller already pays
Full service should stay the same either way
Lower cost should never mean lower protection

If you're buying

How a low commission model works when you're the buyer

Buyers don't pay their realtor directly. The seller sets aside a buyer agent commission in every deal, typically 2.5%, regardless of which agent represents you. A low commission buyer agent takes a flat fee from that amount and returns the rest to you after closing.

Before you sign

Low commission doesn't have to mean low service

Some discount brokerages cut their fee by also cutting what's included. That is the real risk with "low commission" services, not the lower price itself. Confirm exactly what's included before assuming a lower fee means a worse experience.

Full MLS listing with professional photography Some discount listing services only offer a bare-bones or limited-exposure listing
A dedicated agent who personally attends showings Not a rotating team, and not a service that directs you to show your own home
Full negotiation and offer strategy included Not an extra fee on top of the flat fee for writing or negotiating an offer
RECO registration you can verify Check at registrantsearch.reco.on.ca before you commit to anything
