Selling Costs to Prepare For

Most sellers budget for commission and forget everything else. Here's the full picture.

Agent Commission or Flat Fee
The largest selling cost by far, and it's really two separate pieces. A traditional listing agent charges roughly 2.5% for their own side. Sellers also offer a separate buyer agent commission, typically 2% to 2.5%, to attract agents who bring buyers. Together that's the 2.5% to 5% total sellers usually pay. Our flat $4,999 fee replaces the listing side only, you'll still offer a buyer agent commission separately at closing. See the exact math below.
Legal Fees
$1,200 to $1,800
Covers the title transfer, mortgage discharge instructions, and closing adjustments. Get a written estimate from your lawyer before listing.
Mortgage Discharge Fee
$300 to $400
Charged by your lender to formally release their claim on the property once your mortgage is paid off at closing.
Land Transfer Tax
Not a seller cost. This is paid by the buyer only, a common point of confusion. It will not appear on your closing statement.
Staging (Optional)
$2,000 to $6,000
Professional staging for an occupied or vacant home. Virtual staging for photos alone runs $20 to $75 per room, a lower-cost alternative.
Moving Costs
$800 to $3,000+
Professional movers charge by the hour. Book early and avoid end-of-month and weekend dates for the best rates.

Selling a Condo in Toronto

Condo sales come with a document requirement freehold sales don't have, and it can hold up your closing if you leave it too late.

Order your status certificate early
Your condo corporation must issue a status certificate to the buyer's lawyer, covering financials, the reserve fund, and building rules. It costs roughly $100 to $200 and takes about 10 business days to arrive, order it as soon as you accept an offer, not after.
Know your building's numbers before you list
A thin reserve fund or a pending special assessment can affect buyer confidence and financing. Knowing this ahead of time lets us price and market accordingly, rather than being surprised mid-transaction.
Disclose what you're required to disclose
Known building issues, active litigation, or upcoming assessments generally need to be disclosed. Your lawyer will guide you on exactly what applies to your situation.
Staging matters more in a crowded market
Condo buyers often compare several similar units in the same building or area. A clean, well-staged listing stands out against otherwise near-identical inventory.
