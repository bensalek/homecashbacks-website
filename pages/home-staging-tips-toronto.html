Home Staging Tips Toronto

Staging costs money. Here's the real data on whether it pays that back, what to prioritize, and what's safe to skip.

The short answer

Does staging actually pay off?

For most homes, yes, and the data backs it up more strongly than most sellers expect. Nationally, roughly 3 in 10 sellers' agents report staging leads to a meaningful price increase, and close to half report staged homes sell faster. On a $1,000,000 Toronto home, even a conservative 3 to 5% lift is $30,000 to $50,000, well beyond what staging actually costs.

The math on a $1M home
Full staging cost: $2,000 to $6,000
Typical reported price lift: 3% to 5%+
On $1M: $30,000 to $50,000+ in value
Staged homes also tend to sell faster
Physical vs. virtual

Two ways to stage, very different costs

Type Cost Best for
Full physical staging $2,000 – $6,000 Occupied or vacant homes, affects in-person showings too
Virtual staging $20 – $75 per room Vacant homes, photos only, much lower cost
Declutter & deep clean only $0 – $300 Every home, do this regardless of staging budget

Virtual staging is significantly cheaper but only affects photos, not what a buyer walks into at a showing. For occupied homes, decluttering and a deep clean matter more than new furniture, and cost next to nothing.

Where to spend

Prioritize these rooms first

Living room The first real space buyers evaluate. Staged well, it sets the tone for the entire showing.
Primary bedroom Buyers picture themselves living there. A cluttered or overly personal bedroom breaks that.
Kitchen Clear every counter. Buyers judge storage and layout, both are harder to see under clutter.
Entryway First impression, sets expectations for the rest of the showing before a buyer has said a word.

Secondary bedrooms, basements, and laundry rooms matter less. Most professional staging packages skip these entirely to control cost, and buyers weigh them far less heavily than the four rooms above.

What actually matters

Photos and staging are a package deal

Buyers spend most of their time on a listing looking at photos, not reading the description. A well-staged home shot on a phone with poor lighting wastes the staging investment entirely. Stage first, then schedule professional photography, never the other way around.
