What's the Catch With Cashback Realtors?

The honest answer: there is no hidden catch. But there are real questions worth asking — and we will answer all of them.

Is it too good to be true?

It feels that way because the traditional model has normalized agents keeping the full commission. But the commission was never the agent's money to begin with — it comes from the sale price, which the buyer funds when they purchase the home.

On a $1M home at 2.5%, the buyer agent commission is $25,000. A flat-fee cashback realtor charges $4,999 for their service and returns the remaining $20,000. The math works because $4,999 is still meaningful compensation for a transaction that typically takes 2-3 months — not because the service is inferior.

Why traditional agents don't offer thisA traditional agent on a $1M home earns $20,000 to $25,000. There is no financial incentive to change a model that pays that well. The commission structure exists for agents, not buyers.

Do you get less service?

It depends entirely on the cashback realtor. Some models do cut service to justify the lower fee — per-showing charges, limited offer support, or restricted communication. Others provide everything a traditional agent does.

Will they negotiate as hard for me?

This is the most common concern — and the least supported by evidence.

A buyer's agent earns the same commission whether your offer is accepted at $950,000 or $1,050,000. On a 2.5% commission the difference is $2,500 — which gets split with the brokerage, leaving the agent a few hundred dollars. No rational agent is going to undermine your negotiation for a few hundred dollars at the risk of losing your trust, your referrals, and their reputation.

What actually drives good negotiation is market knowledge, offer strategy, and willingness to walk away. None of those depend on how much commission the agent earns.

Common questions

Why don't more realtors offer cashback? +
Because the traditional model pays extremely well and there is no regulatory requirement to share commission. A buyer's agent on a $1M Toronto home earns $20,000 to $25,000. Agents have no financial incentive to change that — only buyers do.
Can I trust that the cashback will actually be paid? +
If it is in your buyer representation agreement, yes — it is a legal obligation of the brokerage, not a goodwill gesture. This is why getting it in writing before any offer is non-negotiable. Verify the agent and brokerage are registered at reco.on.ca.
What if I am not happy with the service? +
With Home Cashbacks, you are never locked into anything. The buyer representation agreement is only signed at offer stage — not before showings. If you are not happy with the service at any point before that, you simply walk away. No cancellation process, no penalty, nothing to get out of.
