Is My Deposit Protected?

A real Ontario brokerage recently had its trust funds misappropriated. Here's exactly what protected buyers when it happened, and what changed afterward.

The short answer

Two layers of protection, by law

Every licensed Ontario brokerage is legally required to hold your deposit in a segregated Real Estate Trust Account, completely separate from the brokerage's own operating funds. On top of that, RECO's Consumer Deposit Insurance Program provides a second layer: coverage up to $200,000 per claimant, with a $4 million cap per event, at no cost to you, if a brokerage commits fraud, theft, or becomes insolvent.

The two layers, at a glance
Segregated trust account, required by law
Deposit insurance up to $200,000 per claim
$4 million cap per event, no deductible
No cost to the buyer, mandatory for every brokerage
This isn't hypothetical

What happened with iPro Realty

iPro Realty, one of Ontario's larger brokerages, had trust funds allegedly misappropriated, roughly $8 million by some reports. This is exactly the scenario the insurance program exists for, and it's a real, recent example of the system being tested.

What actually happened
RECO froze the brokerage's trust accountsImmediately
In-progress transactionsAllowed to close
Consumer deposit claims paid by Dec 2025~$5 million
Eligible claims paid out by Jan 2026100%

No buyer lost their deposit. RECO drew on the insurance program's stability fund to cover the shortfall and ensure full payout.

What changed as a result

Stronger oversight starting in 2026

Directly in response to the iPro Realty case, RECO introduced new financial oversight requirements. Effective October 1, 2026, every Ontario brokerage must submit an annual financial filing covering trust assets, liabilities, and unclaimed trust monies, with a compliance attestation from the broker of record. RECO has also announced plans to introduce monthly trust reconciliation reporting starting in 2027, an even tighter check on how brokerages manage the money they hold on your behalf.

What isn't covered

The one thing this insurance doesn't fix

If a deal falls through and the buyer and seller simply disagree over who should get the deposit back, that's a separate legal dispute, not something this insurance responds to. A brokerage can only release a disputed deposit with a signed mutual release from both parties, or a court order. Your lawyer is who you'd turn to in that situation, not a deposit insurance claim.
