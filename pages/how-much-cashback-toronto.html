Where the money comes from

When a seller lists on MLS they specify a buyer agent commission — typically 2 to 2.5% of the sale price. This money is set aside for whoever represents the buyer at closing. It exists in every deal regardless of which agent you choose.

A traditional agent keeps the entire commission. A cashback realtor takes a flat fee and returns the difference to you. The seller pays the same either way — the only question is whether you see any of it.

💰
You funded this commission when you bought the homeThe buyer agent commission is factored into the sale price. It was always your money — a cashback realtor just gives it back.

Common questions

When do I receive the cashback? +
After closing — typically within a few days of the sale completing. The exact amount is written into your buyer representation agreement before you make any offers, making it a legal obligation of the brokerage.
Is the cashback taxable? +
Generally no. CRA treats cashback from a realtor as a reduction in the purchase price of the home, not income. Consult a tax advisor for investment property purchases.
What if the seller offers less than 2.5% commission? +
Our fee is capped at whatever the seller offered. We disclose the commission before you make any offer. In practice, most GTA listings offer 2 to 2.5% — well above our flat fee on any home above $200,000.
Does cashback affect my mortgage approval? +
No. Cashback is received after closing, not as part of the purchase transaction. It does not affect your mortgage qualification, down payment, or closing costs.
Can I use cashback toward closing costs? +
Indirectly yes. Cashback arrives after closing so it cannot be applied directly to closing costs. But it replenishes your cash reserves immediately after — which for most buyers covers several months of mortgage payments or a significant portion of closing costs paid.
