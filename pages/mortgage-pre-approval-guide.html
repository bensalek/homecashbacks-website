Mortgage Pre-Approval Guide

A pre-approval sets your real budget and locks in a rate while you shop. Here's what it actually gets you, what it doesn't, and what to have ready.

The short answer

A conditional commitment, plus a rate hold

Mortgage pre-approval is a lender's conditional commitment to lend you a specific amount, based on a verified review of your income, credit, and debts, paired with a rate hold that protects your quoted rate for a set period while you shop. It is a strong signal to sellers that you're a serious, ready buyer. It is not, however, a guarantee: final funding still depends on the specific home you buy passing appraisal, and no material change to your finances between now and closing.

What pre-approval gets you
A real, verified budget to shop within
A rate hold, typically 90 to 120 days
Credibility with sellers and listing agents
Protection if rates rise while you search
The distinction that matters

Pre-qualification vs. pre-approval

Pre-qualificationPre-approval
Based onNumbers you self-reportVerified income, credit, and debt documents
Credit checkUsually none, or soft onlyFormal hard credit inquiry
Rate holdNoYes, typically 90 to 120 days
Weight with sellersMinimalTaken seriously in an offer

Pre-qualification is a useful starting point to get a rough sense of your range. Pre-approval is what actually matters once you're ready to make offers in a competitive market.

Before you apply

What to have ready

Government-issued IDA driver's licence or passport.
Recent T4 slips and pay stubsTypically your most recent T4 plus pay stubs from the last 30 days.
Proof of down paymentRecent bank statements, or a signed gift letter if the funds are from family.
Additional documents if self-employedGenerally two years of tax returns and business financials, since income verification is more involved.

With a complete document package, most A-lenders return a pre-approval in 24 to 72 hours. Missing documents are the single biggest cause of delays, so gathering everything before you apply is worth the extra ten minutes.

Protecting your status

Once you're pre-approved, don't do this

Pre-approval is conditional, not final. Between now and closing, avoid taking on new debt, changing jobs, or making large unexplained deposits into your bank account, all of which can trigger a re-review or revoke your approval. If your rate hold expires before you find a home, you'll need to reapply with updated documents and a fresh credit check, so it's worth timing your application to when you're genuinely ready to start shopping, not months ahead of it.
