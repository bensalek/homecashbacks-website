Assignment Sale Toronto

Buying or selling the rights to a pre-construction contract before it closes, not the property itself. Here's how it actually works, and what the HST rules mean for you.

The short answer

You're buying a contract, not a property

An assignment sale happens when the original buyer of a pre-construction unit, the assignor, sells their rights to the purchase agreement to a new buyer, the assignee, before the building is finished and before the original buyer ever takes title. The assignee steps into the assignor's legal position and completes the purchase directly with the builder once the unit closes.

What's actually changing hands
The rights to the original purchase agreement
Responsibility for remaining deposits and payments
Not the property itself, that only transfers at final closing
Requires the builder's written consent to happen at all
The tax rules

HST applies to every assignment sale

Since May 7, 2022, all assignment sales of new or substantially renovated residential property are taxable for HST, regardless of why the original buyer purchased the unit. Before that change, whether HST applied depended on the assignor's original intent, and many individual sellers wrongly assumed they owed nothing. That ambiguity is gone. Ontario HST is 13%, and the assignor is generally responsible for collecting and remitting it, though who actually bears the cost is a matter of negotiation between the parties.

The enhanced rebate follows the original agreement date, not the assignment date
If you're buying an assignment hoping to qualify for the current enhanced HST rebate, check the date of the original Agreement of Purchase and Sale between the first buyer and the builder, not the date you sign the assignment. If that original agreement predates April 1, 2026, the enhanced rebate does not apply, no matter when the assignment itself takes place. Get this confirmed by a lawyer before you rely on it.
How it actually happens

The assignment process, step by step

Confirm the builder will consent, and on what conditions Some builders restrict marketing, others require a construction milestone or proof the assignee qualifies financially.
Negotiate price and who pays what The assignee repays deposits already paid, plus or minus a premium, and the parties agree who covers the builder fee, HST, and adjustments.
Sign the assignment agreement Often built on OREA Form 145 (freehold) or Form 150 (condo), though lawyers frequently draft a tailored agreement since the standard forms rarely cover tax and deposit wording properly.
Apply for and receive builder consent The builder reviews the assignee and signs off, which can take days to a few weeks depending on the developer.
Close with the builder once the unit is ready If closing falls during a condo's interim occupancy period, the assignee takes over the monthly occupancy fee in place of a mortgage until the building registers.

Assignment sales are not standard MLS transactions and come with real legal complexity: developer consent requirements, contract terms that vary significantly by builder, and deposit handling that needs to be properly documented. A real estate lawyer experienced specifically in assignments, not just general closings, is worth the cost here.
